##Healthcare Analytics - Doctor Visits Data Analysis

This project performs an exploratory data analysis (EDA) on a doctor visits dataset to uncover insights and key patterns related to healthcare utilization. The analysis examines the relationships between demographics, health status, insurance types, and the frequency of doctor visits.

### Dataset
The dataset contains information on doctor visits, including demographic details (gender, age, income), health status indicators (illness count, reduced activity days, self-reported health), and insurance information (private insurance, free welfare cards, chronic conditions).

### Analysis Highlights
*   **Data Loading and Initial Inspection**: The notebook begins by loading the dataset and providing an initial overview of its structure, data types, and missing values.
*   **Distribution of Doctor Visits**: Analysis of the overall distribution of doctor visits.
*   **Gender Disparities**: Investigation into average doctor visits based on gender.
*   **Impact of Illness and Activity Reduction**: Examination of how the number of illnesses and reduced activity days due to illness correlate with doctor visits.
*   **Insurance Status and Income**: Analysis of the relationship between private health insurance, free welfare card status, age, and income levels on healthcare utilization.
*   **Chronic Conditions and Health Scores**: Exploration of self-reported health scores in relation to chronic conditions.

### Key Findings
1.  **Doctor Visit Distribution**: A significant portion of the cohort did not visit the doctor, indicating that active healthcare usage is concentrated among a smaller segment.
2.  **Gender Disparities**: Females generally show a higher average number of doctor visits across various income brackets.
3.  **Morbidity & Activity Limitations**: A strong direct correlation exists between the number of concurrent illnesses, days of reduced activity, and healthcare utilization. Higher physical impairment leads to more frequent resource use.
4.  **Insurance Status**: Individuals with private health insurance tend to have a higher baseline rate of visits. Notably, low-income individuals with free government/welfare cards show significantly higher visit rates than those without such coverage.
5.  **Income Dynamics**: Average doctor visits exhibit structural differences across income levels, with low-to-middle income categories displaying greater variability in consultations.

### Conclusion
This analysis confirms that demographic characteristics (gender), baseline illness counts, physical limitations, and structural insurance coverages (private or welfare-based support) are primary drivers of physician visits. These findings are valuable for healthcare providers and policy planners to optimize outreach programs, target chronic illness management, and enhance resource allocation.
